Help&I
In development, not yet available

Help when the power and phones are down.

Press a button and reach people who know you, even in a power cut, with no internet and no mobile signal.

Help&I does not replace 999. In an emergency, call 999 first if you can. Help&I adds to your telecare alarm, your phone and any lone-working arrangements you already have. It does not replace them.

Who it helps

  • Neighbours. Someone nearby knows when you need them, even if your telecare alarm has stopped working in a power cut.
  • People out on their own. Farmers, walkers and lone workers can check in and call for help where there is no signal.
  • Communities. A street, village or valley can keep in touch and look after each other through an outage.
  • Teams. A simple way to call for a hand at a site or an event.

You choose who answers: family, neighbours, a volunteer rota or your own team.

Three buttons

Red: come now Someone is alerted straight away.
Amber: come when you can For things that can wait a little while.
Green: I'm OK now Lets everyone know you are fine.

Use them on your phone, on your watch, or on a small box or pendant that works with no phone at all. After the first press, the phone can ask a few simple follow-up questions so the people coming know what to expect.

What you can count on

  • A person always answers. You are only told help is coming when someone has read your message and said so.
  • You will know if it did not get through. The screen shows a cross, so you can try again or use another way.
  • It keeps working in a power cut. The box runs on its battery and can let people know the power is off, long before it runs low.
  • Check in if you want to. Agree times to press green, and someone is asked to look in if you miss one. It is off unless you turn it on.

We also plan to pass on falls detected by your watch, so that a fall reaches the people who can help even if you cannot press anything.

Why now

Landlines are moving to digital. Many telecare alarms now need mains power and broadband, so they can stop working in a power cut. Mobile masts often lose power within hours. And in much of the countryside there is no signal to begin with.

How it gets through

  1. Over the internet, while it works.
  2. Over the mobile networks, while they work.
  3. Over a local radio network run by the community, which runs on batteries and sunlight and needs no licence.

Your privacy

Messages carry a unit number, never your name, address or health details. You sign a plain sheet that says what is sent and who can see it.

Standards

We are designing Help&I to meet the British standards for lone-worker services and alarm handling. It is not certified yet, and nothing here should be read as if it were.

Open and shared

Help&I is free and open-source. The code and design are on GitHub, and help is welcome: with phone and watch apps, with the electronics, with radio testing, or simply by trying it and telling us what confused you.
