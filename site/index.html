Help&I · In development, not yet available

Get help, even when the
power and phones are down.

Press a button and reach people you have chosen. Help&I tries the internet, then the mobile networks, then a local radio network, so a call can still get through when the usual routes fail.

Help&I does not replace 999. In an emergency, call 999 first if you can. Help&I adds to your telecare alarm, your phone and any lone-working arrangements you already have. It does not replace them.

What works when

Be clear what you have.

Help&I is not magic. What it can reach depends on which parts you use and what your area has set up.

The phone or watch app on its own

Works while your phone has internet or a mobile signal. Like any phone, it cannot send when both are down.

A Help&I box or pendant

Keeps working in a power cut on its battery. It tries the internet, then the mobile networks, then a local radio network if your area has one.

A local radio network

Only exists where local volunteers have set one up, with small solar-powered relays. Without one, a box can only use the internet and mobile networks.

Whichever you use, help only comes if a person you have chosen is there to answer. Help&I tells you when someone has replied. Until then, keep trying other ways if you can.

Who it helps

Set it up for your situation.

You choose who answers: family, neighbours, a volunteer rota or your own team.

Neighbours

Someone nearby knows when you need them, even if your telecare alarm has stopped working in a power cut.

People out on their own

Farmers, walkers and lone workers can check in and call for help where there is no signal.

Communities

A street, village or valley can keep in touch and look after each other through an outage.

Teams

A simple way to call for a hand at a site or an event.

Three buttons

One press says what you need.

Red: come now Someone is alerted straight away.
Amber: come when you can For things that can wait a little while.
Green: I'm OK now Lets everyone know you are fine.

Use them on your phone, on your watch, or on a small box or pendant that works with no phone at all. After the first press, the phone can ask a few simple follow-up questions so the people coming know what to expect.

What you can count on

A person always answers.

Help is confirmed by a person

You are only told help is coming when someone has read your message and said so.

You will know if it did not get through

The screen shows a cross, so you can try again or use another way.

The box keeps working in a power cut

The box runs on its battery and can let people know the power is off, long before it runs low.

Check in if you want to

Agree times to press green, and someone is asked to look in if you miss one. It is off unless you turn it on.

We also plan to pass on falls detected by your watch, so that a fall reaches the people who can help even if you cannot press anything.

Why now

Landlines are moving to digital. Many telecare alarms now need mains power and broadband, so they can stop working in a power cut. Mobile masts often lose power within hours. And in much of the countryside there is no signal to begin with.

How it gets through

Whatever still works.

  1. Over the internet, while it works.
  2. Over the mobile networks, while they work.
  3. Over a local radio network, where your community has set one up. It runs on batteries and sunlight and needs no licence.

Your privacy

Messages carry a unit number, never your name, address or health details. You sign a plain sheet that says what is sent and who can see it.

Standards

We are designing Help&I to meet the British standards for lone-worker services and alarm handling. It is not certified yet, and nothing here should be read as if it were.

Open and shared

Built in the open.

Help&I is free and open-source, and help is welcome: hardware development, software development, testing, advice, or simply an idea. Tell us how you would like to help.
