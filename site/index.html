Press a button and reach people you have chosen, over the internet, the mobile networks, or a local radio network*.

Help&I does not replace 999. In an emergency, call 999 first if you can. Help&I adds to your telecare alarm, your phone and any lone-working arrangements you already have. It does not replace them.

Who it helps

Set it up for your situation.

You choose who answers: family, neighbours, a volunteer rota or your own team. Choose a situation to read more.

Neighbours

Someone nearby knows when you need them, even if your telecare alarm has stopped working in a power cut.* (needs a local radio network)More

A household chooses a few people to answer: family, neighbours or a local volunteer rota. If the first person does not reply, the request goes to the next. It sits alongside a telecare alarm, not instead of it.

People out on their own

Farmers, walkers and lone workers can check in and call for help.More

Set check-in times and choose who is told if you miss one. Help&I needs a mobile signal, or a local radio network within range*, to get a message out. In remote country with neither, it cannot get through, and we are looking at satellite messaging for those places.

Communities

A street, village or valley can keep in touch and look after each other through an outage.* (needs a local radio network)More

A community can set up its own radio relays, mains powered with battery and optional solar backup, and a rota of volunteers to answer. In a long outage, responders see one summary of who is on battery and who has asked for help, not a flood of alarms.

Teams

A simple way to call for a hand at a site or an event.More

A site, farm or event can give each person the app or a pendant and make the team the responders. Button labels can change, so green might mean “checked in”. It is not a certified lone-worker service.

Three buttons

One press says what you need.

Red: help Starts a call for help to the people you chose.
Amber: need a hand For things that can wait a little while.
Green: I'm OK Lets everyone know you are fine.

Use them on your phone, on your watch, or on a small box or pendant. After the first press, the phone can ask a few simple follow-up questions so the people coming know what to expect.

Check in if you want to

Agree times to press green, and someone is asked to look in if you miss one. It is off unless you turn it on.

We also plan to pass on falls detected by your watch, so that a fall reaches the people who can help even if you cannot press anything.

Why now

Landlines are moving to digital. Many telecare alarms now need mains power and broadband, so they can stop working in a power cut. Mobile masts often lose power within hours.

How it gets through

Whatever still works.

  1. Over your home Wi-Fi, straight to the others of your group at home, with no internet.
  2. Over the internet, while it works.
  3. Over your mobile phone network. An iPhone cannot send a text by itself, so if the other ways have failed it offers you one to tap Send on.
  4. Over other mobile phone networks, if your phone has a SIM that can use them.
  5. Over a local radio network*, where your community has set one up. It runs on mains power, with battery and optional solar backup, and needs no licence.

Your privacy

The people who answer see your name, from their own list of the people they look after. The call for help itself carries only a code, so a stranger listening cannot tell who needed help. Anything you type is locked so that only the people answering can read it.

Standards

We are designing Help&I to meet the British standards for lone-worker services and alarm handling. It is not certified yet, and nothing here should be read as if it were.

Be clear what you have

*Help&I is not magic.

What it can reach depends on which parts you use and what your area has set up. Everything marked * needs a local radio network.

The app on its own

Works while your phone has internet or a mobile signal, and at home over your Wi-Fi to the others of your group there. Like any phone, it cannot send when all of those are down.

A Help&I box or pendant

A small radio with the three buttons. It keeps running on its battery in a power cut, and sends over the local radio network where there is one, or through your phone when the phone is nearby. Your phone goes out with you; the box stays at home.

A local radio network

Only exists where local volunteers have set one up. Its relays run on mains power, with battery and optional solar backup. Without one, a box can only send through your phone.

Help&I tells you when someone has replied, and shows a cross if your message could not get through. Help only comes if a person you have chosen is there to answer.

Questions

Questions people ask.

Can I get Help&I yet?

Answer

Not yet. It is being built and tested, and nothing here is ready to rely on. Join the waitlist to hear when it is.

Does it replace 999 or my telecare alarm?

Answer

No. Help&I adds to 999, NHS 111, telecare and any lone-working procedure you already have. In an emergency, call 999 first if you can.

Do I need the box?

Answer

No. The app on its own works while your phone has internet or a mobile signal, and at home over your Wi-Fi to the others of your group there. The box adds buttons that work without a phone and keep working in a power cut, and the local radio network where there is one.

What happens in a power cut?

Answer

The box runs on its battery and can tell the people you chose that the power is off, long before it runs low. It sends over the local radio network where there is one, or through your phone while the phone has a signal. Without either, a message cannot get out, and the box shows a cross.

Who answers when I press the button?

Answer

The people you chose: family, neighbours, a volunteer rota or your own team. There is no call centre. If the first person does not reply, the request goes to the next. Help only comes if one of them is there to answer.

How will I know someone is coming?

Answer

You are only told help is coming when one of your people has read your message and said so. If your message could not get through, the screen shows a cross, so you can try again or use another way.

What is sent, and who can see it?

Answer

The call for help carries only a code, so a stranger listening cannot tell who needed help. The people who answer see your name from their own list. Anything you choose to add, such as a short message, “Fallen” or where you are, is locked so that only the people answering can read it.

Which phones and watches will it work on?

Answer

iPhone and Apple Watch first. Android is planned, and a volunteer Android developer would help it come sooner.

Is there a local radio network near me?

Answer

Only where local volunteers have set one up. It uses small radio relays that need no licence, on mains power with battery and optional solar backup. A community can build its own; tell us if yours would like to.

Is it certified?

Answer

Not yet. We are designing it to meet the British standards for lone-worker services and alarm handling, and nothing here should be read as if it were certified.

I have another question.

Answer
